# juuri.me — text style guide

For the agent working on this site. This covers **words only**: copy, headings, labels, microtext.
Nothing here is about layout, colour, spacing or code. Do not change structure to satisfy a copy rule.

---

## The voice, in one line

Plain statements of fact, one idea each, no selling. The reader decides what is impressive.

This is a CFO's page, not a personal brand. It should read like someone who has nothing to prove
and is simply telling you what is true. Every sentence that reaches for effect weakens the ones
that don't.

---

## The seven rules

### 1. One idea per sentence

If a sentence contains "and" joining two different thoughts, split it or cut one.

- Weak: "As Chief Financial Officer, I hold full P&L responsibility for finance, strategy and transformation at Finland's largest nuclear power producer, generating roughly 30% of the country's electricity, and report directly to the Board and ownership."
- Better: "I hold full P&L responsibility for finance, strategy and transformation at Finland's largest nuclear power producer. TVO generates roughly 30% of the country's electricity. I report directly to the Board and ownership."

Three facts, three sentences. Easier to read and each one lands.

### 2. No lift lines

Cut any sentence whose job is to make the reader feel something rather than know something.
These are the LinkedIn-guru sentences and they are the single biggest thing making the page
feel off.

Cut or rewrite:
- "Few investments compound like getting this right early."
- "That is most of the point."
- "The discipline carries back into the working week."

Each of these is a conclusion drawn for the reader. State the fact and stop. If the fact is
good, the reader gets there without help.

### 3. No self-promotion adjectives

Remove: *strategic, proven, passionate, driven, results-oriented, extensive, seasoned, robust,
world-class, cutting-edge, leading.*

Scale and seniority should come only from concrete nouns and numbers: the euro figures, the
percentage of national electricity, the years, the company names. Never from an adjective.

### 4. No em-dashes

Use a comma, a full stop, or a colon. Never `—` or `--`.

- Wrong: "The work is long-horizon — capital programmes in the billions."
- Right: "The work is long-horizon: capital programmes in the billions."

### 5. Say it once

The page currently repeats itself. Each fact belongs in exactly one place.

- "Fifteen years of senior finance leadership across power, renewables and clean technology"
  appears in the intro and again under **04 Track record**. Keep it in the intro, delete it from 04.
- "Replies within a week" appears in both **06** and **07**. Keep it once.
- **06 Get in touch** and **07 Get in touch** are the same section twice. Merge them.

### 6. Fragments are allowed, but commit to them

The clipped style works: "Siemens Wind, then Neste." "Billund, Rotterdam, Geneva."
The problem is mixing fragments and full sentences inside one paragraph, which reads as
unfinished rather than deliberate.

Rule: a block is either all fragments or all full sentences. Do not alternate within a block.

Also fix the broken ones. "Training in the winter constant, and almost all of it is shared with
family and friends" is not a sentence. Rewrite as: "Training is constant through the winter.
Almost all of it is shared with family and friends."

### 7. Never write a demand

Calls to action state what is available, not what the reader must do.

- Wrong: "Get in touch today", "Let's connect", "Reach out to discuss how I can help."
- Right: "Direct email: joni@juuri.me. Replies within a week."
- Right: "Open to senior international roles. Three-month notice."

The existing closing lines are correct. Keep that register everywhere.

## Formatting conventions

### Numbers and money

Pick one format and apply it everywhere. The page currently mixes `€500m`, `€550M`, `€130M+`
and "multi-billion-euro" in the same section.

- Money: `€500M`, `€1.2B`. Capital M and B, no space, euro sign first.
- Approximations: "roughly €130M", not "€130M+".
- Percentages: `30%`, no space.
- Years as ranges: `2019–2022` (en dash), and `2025–present` for the current role.
- Spell out numbers under ten in prose: "Seven of those abroad", "Three programmes".
  Use digits in labels, figures and dates.

### Headings

Section titles are sentence case and end with a full stop. This is deliberate. Keep it consistent:

> Current role. · Seven years abroad. · Current priorities. · Track record. · Outside the role.

Micro-labels above or beside content are ALL CAPS, no full stop:

> ROLE · BASED · CAREER MAP · ELSEWHERE · LEG 01 · NEXT GENERATION · EMAIL · LOCATION

Do not mix the two. A label is never a sentence and a heading is never shouted.

### Lists and tags

Tag rows (HYROX, SPINNING, CYCLING, GYM, TENNIS) stay single words, all caps, no punctuation
between them. Do not let these grow past six items.

### Company and place names

Write them as the organisation writes them. `TVO` on second reference, `Teollisuuden Voima Oyj`
once in full. `Neste`, `Siemens Wind`, `Fortum`, not "Siemens Wind Power A/S". No job-title
inflation in the track record: the title as it was on the contract.

## Person and tense

- First person, present tense, for the current role: "I hold", "I report", "I serve".
- Past tense for history, without "I" where the fragment style carries it: "Siemens Wind from 2015."
- Never third person. No "Joni is a finance leader who...".
- Never "we" unless it genuinely means TVO.

---

## Length

- Intro block: 60 words maximum.
- Section intro under a heading: one or two sentences.
- Any single body paragraph: four sentences maximum. If it needs more, it is two paragraphs.
- Card descriptions (the priorities blocks): 25 words maximum, one fact and its scale.

Shorter is almost always the fix. When a passage is not working, cut the second half of it
before rewriting the first.

---

## The test

Read the sentence aloud and ask: would he say this to a board member across a table?

If it sounds like a pitch, cut it. If it sounds like a fact, keep it.
